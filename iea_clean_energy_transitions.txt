AUTHORITY: International Energy Agency (IEA Paris)
CATEGORY: Global Demand Management & Decarbonization Pathways

1. Demand-Side Flexibility and Load Shifting:
- Shifting discretionary electrical loads away from grid peak hours lowers overall system stress, dampens costly spot electricity market prices, and maximizes the absorption of variable renewable generation (solar PV).
- Implementing smart building energy management systems (BEMS) enables real-time sub-metering, automated setpoint modulation, and proactive fault detection across sub-distribution boards.

2. Thermal Pre-Cooling and Building Thermal Inertia:
- Pre-cooling educational and institutional building envelopes during low-tariff early morning hours (06:00 to 08:30) allows facilities to curtail active chiller demand during high-tariff afternoon peak windows (11:00 to 15:00) without compromising occupant comfort.
- Integrating thermal energy storage (ice banks or chilled water tanks) decouples cooling generation from cooling utilization, shaving peak HVAC demand by 25% to 40%.

3. Motor Systems & Variable Frequency Drives (VFDs):
- Motors, pumps, and ventilation fans represent over 40% of building mechanical power draw.
- Fitting VFDs on air handling units (AHUs) and water circulation pumps allows output modulation matching variable thermal loads, reducing pumping energy consumption by up to 50% through Affinity Laws (power is proportional to the cube of speed).
